Both formulations of the paper's resolvability criterion (Sect. 4.2)

Schulze states two versions of resolvability. Formulation #1 (4.2.1) says the proportion of profiles without a unique winner tends to zero as the number of voters grows, which is proved by reducing to the combinatorial claim below: profiles where two links happen to have equivalent strength become vanishingly rare as the electorate grows.
Formulation #2 (4.2.2) is the ballot-adding version: for every alternative a among several winners, one can add a single new ballot so that a becomes the unique winner. This file develops only the combinatorial core underlying Formulation #1; the box below states precisely how much of it is proved here. The rest lives in two later files: CriticalLinkN.v proves the combinatorial core of Formulation #1 (pairwise distinct link strengths force a unique winner), and ResolvabilityBallotN.v proves Formulation #2 from the ballots of BallotN.v.

Resolvability (Section 4.2) — the resolution step only

Schulze's 4.2.1 argues that two distinct winners a and b force a tie P_D[a,b] ≈ P_D[b,a] (4.2.1.3), and then rules that out when no two links have equivalent strength. What follows is the contrapositive of the first half: a winner that is never tied in the closure beats everyone, hence is the unique winner.
The second half — that distinct LINK strengths force an untied closure — is distinct_links_unique_winner in CriticalLinkN.v. The paper proves it by locating the weakest link of the strongest path and splitting both paths there; that file replaces the path surgery by threshold and cut arguments on the closure. What remains unformalised is the criterion as literally stated: 4.2.1 is a limit over profiles as the electorate grows, and has no counterpart here.
A winner that is nowhere tied in the closure beats everyone.
  Theorem untied_winner_is_strict {R : BoundedSemiring.type}
    (Htotal : ∀ x y : R, x + y = x ∨ x + y = y)
    (M : @Matrix Node R) (a : Node)
    (Hnoties : ∀ b, b ≠ a → mat_star M a b ≠ mat_star M b a) :
    schulze_winner M a → strict_winner M a.
  Proof.
    intros Hwin X HX.
    unfold schulze_beats, beats.
    destruct (Htotal (mat_star M a X) (mat_star M X a)) as [Hc | Hc].
    -
      split.
      + unfold Orel. rewrite addC. exact Hc.
      + intro Heq. exact (Hnoties X HX (eq_sym Heq)).
    -
      exfalso. apply (Hwin X HX). split; [exact Hc |].
      exact (Hnoties X HX).
  Qed.

…and is therefore the only winner: the resolution step of 4.2.1.
  Corollary untied_winner_unique {R : BoundedSemiring.type}
    (Htotal : ∀ x y : R, x + y = x ∨ x + y = y)
    (M : @Matrix Node R) (a : Node)
    (Hnoties : ∀ b, b ≠ a → mat_star M a b ≠ mat_star M b a) :
    schulze_winner M a → ∀ w, schulze_winner M w → w = a.
  Proof.
    intros Hwin w Hw.
    destruct (fin_eq_dec w a) as [Heq | Hne]; [exact Heq | exfalso].
    exact (strict_winner_excludes_others M a w
             (untied_winner_is_strict Htotal M a Hnoties Hwin) Hne Hw).
  Qed.
